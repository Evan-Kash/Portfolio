Lava Tower + Color Switch

About

A puzzle-platformer about climbing a four-sided tower ahead of rising lava. You only ever see one face of the tower: walk off its edge and the tower turns to show the next one, so whatever is around a corner stays out of sight until you look for it.

Color switch. Platforms and doors are neutral, red, or blue, and the colored ones only exist while you are that color. Once you've picked up both colors, a key swaps between them, which can bring the way up into view or make the floor under you vanish.

Scouting. A minimap of the whole tower shows the color you're not, so the two views together are the full picture. Turning it to look at another face freezes you while the lava keeps rising, so every look around a corner costs height.

What I built

  • The tower: four faces that turn when the player reaches an edge, with the player carried around the corner onto the next face and only the front face solid. It runs on a publish/subscribe event bus, so the tower, player, camera, and UI never call each other directly.
  • Player movement: running, jumping, climbing ropes and ladders, and doors that pair up by color and symbol and take you to their partner.
  • The rising lava with its warning toast, and the win and game-over screens.
  • The color mechanic: pickups, the switch key with its on-screen prompt, camera culling layers that draw only your color, and a check that stops a switch from trapping you inside a platform.
  • The minimap: a second camera orbiting the tower into a render texture, showing the opposite color, framed in the color it shows, with markers for the lava and the player.
  • The level: a tutorial that introduces one color per pickup, then blind corners and routes that trade a short, risky climb against a long, safe one.
  • Iteration from feedback: the minimap and the color mechanic both answer the playtest critique that the first version's rotation rarely asked the player to make a decision.
